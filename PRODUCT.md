# From useful MVP to a business

## Product thesis

Gap-year graduates need an achievable next commitment, not an endless list of activities. The core loop is **direction → verified shortlist → weekly action → evidence → reflection**. The differentiator to test is graduate-aware eligibility combined with follow-through.

## Five-minute demo

1. Search from the homepage or click a floating example. No profile is required.
2. Open an activity and show its output, eligibility, official source, and three next steps.
3. Save its checklist, complete one step, and refresh to demonstrate persistence.
4. Add an example Biology profile, age 17, gap-year graduate. Show the tailored results and enrollment exclusions.
5. Compare two options and log real work in the evidence log. The longer plan remains optional.
6. Export a workspace backup or personal target dates when useful.

The public demo does not depend on a paid API or a running local server. The guide is structured coaching, not a live language model.

## First pilot

Recruit a small cohort of actual gap-year graduates through existing school or counselor relationships. Start with one region and a few majors so eligibility can be checked well. Ask about their last real attempt to find and finish an opportunity before pitching features. Do not claim demand, retention, admissions impact, or revenue until observed.

For an initial four-week pilot, measure with participant consent:

- Activation: creates a profile, saves an eligible opportunity, and schedules a first task.
- Follow-through: completes meaningful work and records evidence in a later week.
- Trust: number of incorrect or unclear eligibility claims discovered.
- Value: whether a student or counselor would keep using the workflow, and why.

Targets and pricing should follow interviews and usage, not invented benchmarks. Admission outcomes are slow and confounded; do not treat them as a causal product metric.

## Next implementation priorities, after validation

1. **Opportunity operations:** a review queue, expiry rules, source-change checks, and wider graduate-specific coverage. Add manual tracker entries for specific events discovered through directories.
2. **Account-backed persistence:** authenticated storage with per-user access policies, backup recovery, consent and retention controls appropriate for younger users, and cross-device sync. Local storage is sufficient for this demo, not the final account system.
3. **Planning flexibility:** custom projects and tasks, task carry-over, mentor comments, and explicit graduation-date/country rule checks for programs that publish them.
4. **Distribution:** test a counselor workflow and curated cohort guides before spending on paid acquisition.
5. **AI only where useful:** evaluate whether free-form feedback improves decisions. Keep factual program information anchored to reviewed sources. Add public inference only when usage, privacy, and compute costs are understood.

Funding readiness depends on evidence of a real user problem, repeated use, distribution, and a sustainable business. This release provides a functioning demonstration and a foundation for that work; it does not establish those outcomes.
